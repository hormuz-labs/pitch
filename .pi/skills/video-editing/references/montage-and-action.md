# Montage, cinematic footage and physical action

Start with the requested story and the actual shots. A highlight, trailer,
physical demonstration and music-led montage have different content coverage
from a faithful screen tutorial. The brief may call for selection rather than
preserving every source interval.

Use shot/action boundaries, emotional beats and music structure where relevant.
Silence or stillness may be suspense, composition or a deliberate hold; the screen
prepass is not a default. Transcribe when dialogue carries meaning, but do not
force camera emphasis onto every spoken noun. Retain complete essential actions,
their setup and result when the request is instructional or evidentiary.

Preserve physical direction, eyelines, contact points and intelligible cause and
effect. A fixed crop is suitable only while the subject remains inside it. For
movement beyond the crop, widen or use genuinely supported tracking/reframing;
do not pretend the current viewport tool tracks a subject.

Use transitions and speed changes only when they serve the treatment. Speed
changes resample frames; they do not supply optical flow. Consider motion blur,
cadence, audio transients and footage quality. Dialogue cuts need word/meaning
review, even when music sets the surrounding rhythm.

For gameplay, preserve the HUD and spatial setup necessary to understand the
highlight. For generated footage, inspect continuity and artifacts rather than
assuming the requested prompt was faithfully rendered. Never invent product UI
as a replacement for a missing real interaction.
