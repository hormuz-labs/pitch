# Speech, interviews and people

The edit preserves a speaker's meaning and the viewer's sense of a conversation.
A pause can carry emotion, hesitation, a reaction or a turn change even when
neither the transcript nor a coarse motion detector marks an event.

Do not apply the screen-demo automatic dead-time/preserve-every-frame policy.
Inspect/transcribe first and choose cuts from complete thoughts and the brief.
Remove setup or repetition only when meaning, qualifications and speaker order
remain intact. Preserve breaths/reactions where they make the speech human.
Keep speech intelligible at natural speed; avoid blanket acceleration of pauses.

Use a stable composition with headroom, gaze and relevant gestures. A restrained
punch-in can mark a thought or conceal a justified edit; do not zoom at each noun
as if locating UI controls. Camera emphasis follows meaning/expression, not an
inventory of every object mentioned. For multicamera material choose available
angles without inventing continuity or offscreen reactions.

For webcam over a tutorial, read narrated-screen for the UI sequence. Keep the
speaker inset off the active controls/text. Align sources from real sync points
and inspect drift at both ends; an initial offset is not sufficient evidence.

Review cut sides with audio: sentence meaning, breaths, words, room tone, gaze,
gesture and lip sync. A cropped still or waveform correlation cannot certify
conversational rhythm. For a short social excerpt, the requested argument/topic
defines content coverage rather than full-source preservation.
