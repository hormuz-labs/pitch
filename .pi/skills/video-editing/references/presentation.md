# Presentations, charts and narrated documents

Attention follows the spoken reference and the time needed to understand the
material. A static, silent slide may be a necessary reading or comparison hold.

Inspect the actual rendered page, its labels and any existing callouts. Establish
page order and required narration/reading intervals before trimming. Preserve
approved storyboard coverage. Use a prepass only with those intervals protected;
do not classify a page as disposable because its pixels stop changing.

Make a reference map for each substantive spoken statistic, chart feature or
diagram step. Focus or highlight the actual item as it is named, using word/phrase
timing. Keep axes, units, comparison series, footnotes and the relationships needed
to interpret it. If a comparison spans distant regions, use a wider composition
rather than alternating tight crops that conceal the relationship.

Group related points into one stable hold or deliberate pan. Avoid zooming at
every bullet. Use full-page orientation on a page change, then detail when needed.
Do not duplicate highlights already baked into a recording. A prerecorded slide
annotation and a new camera move should have a single clear attentional purpose.

Check spoken item → visible label → intended inference in the rendered output.
Readability at final delivery size and sufficient dwell are acceptance criteria.
Do not assert an unreadable statistic from OCR alone or fabricate a target when
the page contradicts the narration.
