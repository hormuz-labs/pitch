# Targeted correction

The user's selected operation and interval define scope. Inspect the affected
source and its boundaries; do not rerun a full-edit workflow because the file
happens to be a demo or interview.

- A level-only change needs the intended track/stem, gain/mix handling and audio
  checks. It does not justify new zooms, automatic dead-time cuts or retiming.
- A selected blur/crop needs region and temporal coverage in output coordinates.
  Inspect movement throughout the interval; endpoints alone do not prove privacy.
- An exact trim uses the stated time basis and boundaries. Preserve words and
  requested range; clarify genuinely conflicting requirements rather than
  silently changing the selected span.
- A zoom-timing correction reads the relevant footage branch and camera reference,
  then revises the affected emphasis and dependent mappings. Preserve unrelated
  approved edits.

Use a direct documented `pitch media ffmpeg` operation when simpler than a full
plan. Use the saved editable plan/stems where available. A mixed audio track does
not expose music and voice separately. Do not promise to isolate a bed by merely
lowering the entire mix.

Check the affected range and both boundaries, plus metadata/duration as relevant.
Broaden review when the operation changes downstream timing, dimensions or shared
audio, not by default. State exactly what changed and any scope limitation.
