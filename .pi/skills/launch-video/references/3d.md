# Depth: three.js scenes

Use 3D only where depth explains something physical or spatial: layers, parts,
placement, scale, how a thing goes together. A flat idea gains nothing from a
third dimension, and a rotating device is only one subject among many.

Query only the 3D sections you need (`pitch motion schema --types device-3d`,
`--section "real 3d, lottie and rive in a custom type"`). Animation stays on the
shot's seekable timeline through `ShotKit.three`. Match the object's landing
and the camera's framing to the next shot's handoff box.

## Craft

- **Light it like a product shoot:** a soft key from one side and a rim behind
  that separates the silhouette from the ground. Flat ambient light reads as a
  placeholder.
- **Camera at about 35–55° of elevation.** Never look straight down on a large
  surface, and never end tight on a flat face; both hide the depth that
  justified 3D.
- **Parts meet:** nothing floats off what it attaches to, no gaps at joins, no
  untextured black glass, no visibly repeating texture, no square posts where a
  round part belongs.
- **Buildings and spaces** read best as a premium architectural model: real
  materials, a base. Fake photorealism reads uncanny.
- **Labels are HTML,** never text inside the scene. Project their anchor every
  frame (`vector.project(camera)` in the same hook that renders) and place the
  label from it, so it stays sharp while the camera moves.
- **Match the brand ground.** The `ShotKit.three` canvas is transparent, so the
  shot's CSS ground shows through. A `scene.background`, a backdrop mesh or tone
  mapping (ACES shifts every colour) moves it off the brand value: compare a
  background pixel in a review frame with the brand colour and adjust until
  they match.

## Motion that must look real

When the object does what the real product does (opens, folds, docks):

- **Measure it** from the product's own footage (`references/reference-video.md`):
  read the angle or position off frames at known times and write keys
  (seconds → value). Those measurements are the curve. A real fold is fastest
  mid-swing and dwells near edge-on before it lands; a generic ease is not.
- **Interpolate keys with a monotone cubic** with zero end slopes; blending an
  ease over them moves the dwell and adds a hiccup.
- **Lock the camera during the move.** A reframe that stops on the contact
  frame reads as a bump; reframe after the landing. Contact is dead still.
- **Start lifts with a smootherstep** (`x*x*x*(x*(x*6-15)+10)`); a cubic
  ease-out leaves its first frame at full speed and pops. Stagger parts by a
  few milliseconds.
- **Screens stay lit** on a moving part and blur rather than switch off; a
  screen that continues across a cut shows the same layout on both sides.
- **A one-frame glint** is fixed by widening the light so the highlight builds
  over several frames, not by dimming it.
- **Motion blur** is the film's `SHOTS.render` block (`shutter`, `samples`;
  `--section "render and grade"`). It applies to the whole film and multiplies
  export time by `samples`: use it only when a fast move needs it.
