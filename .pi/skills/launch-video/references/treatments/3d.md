# 3D product animation

Use 3D where depth explains something physical or spatial: layers, parts,
placement, scale, how a thing is assembled. A rotating device is only one
possible subject; a flat idea gains nothing from a third dimension. Choose
geometry, material, lighting and camera movement for the product idea, with a
focal object whose movement feels consistent with its material and weight.

Before implementation, query only the relevant 3D section of `pitch motion
schema` and study a suitable three.js/devices/morph example with `pitch effects`.
Keep animation on the seekable shot timeline, using the documented ShotKit
integration. Consult `references/effects.md` when adapting source. Match the
object's landing and camera framing to the next shot rather than adding a
persistent ornament to connect unrelated scenes.

## Craft

- Light it like a product shoot: a soft key from one side and a rim light
  behind to separate the silhouette from the ground. Flat ambient light reads as
  a placeholder.
- Keep the camera at a three-quarter elevation. Looking straight down on a
  large surface flattens it, and ending tight on a flat face hides the depth
  that justified 3D.
- Parts meet: no floating pieces, gaps at joins, untextured black glass or
  visibly repeating textures.
- Labels are HTML, not text inside the scene. Project their anchor each frame
  (`vector.project(camera)` inside the same frame hook that renders) and place
  the label from it, so it stays sharp and readable while the camera moves.
- The `ShotKit.three` canvas is transparent, so the shot's CSS ground shows
  through. A `scene.background`, a backdrop mesh or a tone mapping you add
  (ACES shifts every colour) is lit and moves away from the brand value:
  compare a background pixel in a review frame with it.
- Toy giveaways: parts that float off what they attach to, square posts where
  a round part belongs, open seams at ridges and edges, plinth-like trim. A
  maquette style (real materials, a base board) reads premium where fake
  photorealism reads uncanny.

## Motion that must look real

When the object does something the real product does (opens, folds, docks):

- Measure it from the product's own footage (`references/reference-video.md`):
  read the angle or position off frames at known times and write keys
  (seconds → value). Real mechanisms are rarely a smoothstep; a fold is fastest
  mid-swing and dwells near edge-on before it lands.
- Interpolate the keys with a monotone cubic with zero end slopes, not a
  generic ease blended over them; blending moves the dwell and adds a hiccup.
- Lock the camera during the move. A reframe that stops on the contact frame
  reads as a bump; reframe after the landing. Contact is dead still: no jolt,
  barely any rebound.
- A lift or separation that starts with a cubic ease-out leaves its first
  frame at full speed and pops. Use a smootherstep
  (`x*x*x*(x*(x*6-15)+10)`) and stagger parts by a few milliseconds.
- Screens are half the realism: a display on a moving part stays lit and blurs
  rather than switching off, and a screen that continues across a cut shows the
  same layout pixel for pixel on both sides.
- Fix a one-frame glint by widening the light so the highlight builds over
  several frames; dimming it removes the cue that the angle is changing.
- Motion blur is the film's `SHOTS.render` block (`shutter`, `samples`; see the
  schema's "Render and grade"). It applies to the whole film and multiplies
  export time by `samples`, so ask for it only when a fast move needs it.
