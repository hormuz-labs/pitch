# Studying a reference video

When the user supplies a video whose motion they like, study it before writing
`direction.md`. A reference is for understanding, never for copying: do not
reuse its footage, logos, layout, words or music.

1. `pitch media probe --file <file>` for its length and frame rate.
2. Overview: `pitch video frames --source <file> --times '[0,1,2,…]' --contact-sheet`
   at about one frame a second (twelve frames per call). Find where the picture turns over.
3. Around each transition worth learning, take a dense sheet: eight frames
   inside one second (every 0.125s) spanning the change. A transition read from
   two settled frames is a guess; the mechanism is in the frames between.
4. Write a short note in `direction.md` per reference: its key moment, how that
   moment works (what leads, what carries across the cut, how the speed changes),
   and how the idea could serve this product with this product's own objects.

Measure before describing. If a timing matters to the film, read it off the
sheet's timestamps instead of estimating from playback. What a reference
teaches is a principle (the wipe bar becomes the phone); its shapes, palette
and durations stay with it.
